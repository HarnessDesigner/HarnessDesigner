## FILES

#### __init__.py

Package docstring only — explains that most of this package's former `Add*Handler` classes have been migrated onto the objects themselves (see `add_handlers/`) and removed; what remains here is view-agnostic logic those replacements (and `drag_handlers/`/`rotation_handlers/`) still call into: part-compatibility lookups, wire fork/merge, terminal/transition/seal placement geometry, snap-probe building blocks, and the peg-board rope-pull reconciliation solver. No code of its own.

#### bundle_diameter.py

The shared bundle-diameter rule (3D + peg-board) — a bundle's diameter is `max(wire-driven diameter, every attached transition branch's own min_dia)`, and growing/shrinking a branch's own project-level `diameter` to meet the bundle is symmetric (not a one-way ratchet). Wire-driven diameter uses the real concentric-packing figure when one exists, else an "unorganized packing" area-sum estimate scaled by a fudge factor. `wires_diameter(bundle)` is the read-only public form of the wire-driven diameter (mm), used by the circuit solver; unlike `effective_diameter` it never grows a transition branch.

#### bundle_layout_handler.py

Inserts a new interior `BundleLayout` waypoint into a bundle's 3D path at the clicked position/segment (`_create_bundle_layout_on_bundle`), reached only from a bundle's own "Add Handle" context-menu action.

#### bundle_topology.py

`merge_bundles` — the one bundle-topology operation that survives the waypoint model (bends are just interior waypoints, not forks): merging two independently-placed bundles dragged together end-to-end into one row, mirroring `wire_topology.merge_wires`.

#### concentric_handler.py

Which concentric a bundle / transition branch uses (2026-10-25): `find_concentric_for_bundle` / `find_concentric_for_branch` (read-only, `None` if there is none) and `concentric_for_bundle` / `concentric_for_branch` (the same, making an empty concentric when there is none). A connected bundle and branch share ONE concentric (and so one widget) by LOOKUP, never by writing ids: a bundle uses its own, else the first one among the branches plugged into either end; a branch uses its own, else its bundle's. Where BOTH already have one (made separately before they were joined) each keeps its own -- nothing merges concentrics, so that case still has two widgets. Wires in a concentric (NEW 2026-10-25): `add_wire` (the drop of a wire from a peg-board wire table onto a concentric section widget lands here), `replan`, `needs_plan` and `build_section` (the adapter from the database to `ui/concentric_section`'s `SectionSpec` + `WireDetail`). A wire is "in" a concentric when a `pjt_concentric_wires` row of one of its layers names it; a new wire goes in the OUTERMOST layer, and a concentric with no layers gets layer 0. **Layer diameters are deliberately left alone**: `bundle_diameter._wires_diameter` takes the outermost layer's `diameter` as the bundle's size the moment its concentric has any layer, so a placeholder or a made-up figure would silently resize the bundle in 3D and on the peg board; a new first layer gets the diameter the bundle has right now (its wires' estimate, else the attached branch's `diameter`, else the wire's own OD) and an existing layer's diameter is never rewritten. **`replan` is INTERIM**: it packs every cable as tightly as it can in a circle (`ui/concentric_section/packing.py`) and stores each cable's place in its `point2d`; it does not follow the MIL-STD-339 layer rules (CONCENTRIC_TWIST_DESIGN.md sections 4-8) -- when that solver exists it replaces `replan` and nothing else here changes. It also overwrites every cable's place on each add (a manual drag of a cable is not saved yet either). **Cables are drawn from what the catalog knows** (`_cable_for`): there is no jacket flag and no jacket / shield thickness, so a coaxial or shielded part gets a shield and a jacket (`_SHIELD_FRACTION` / `_JACKET_FRACTION` of its OD), any other wire none (several conductors = a twisted group); the conductor colour is the plating row's own `color`, the insulation / stripe colours the part's. The wire details (`_detail_for`) take the current and temperature from `circuit.solve()` (one solve per circuit per build) and are `None` for a wire in no circuit. Nothing checks that a dropped wire actually runs through the bundle the concentric belongs to. Checked 2026-10-25 against fake rows only (no real database): no duplicates, layer diameters untouched, no overlapping cables, the cable kinds, the details.

#### handler_base.py

`HandlerBase` (~290 lines) — captures mainframe/part_id/camera/ptables state for a handler; mouse-capture lifecycle (`capture_position()`/`release_capture()`/`ignore_next_input()`, where `release_capture` raises `NotImplementedError` so subclasses must implement placement); and orientation-alignment helpers used to snap a newly placed accessory to the face of the housing/cavity it's being dropped into: `obb_face_direction()` (outward-normal for one OBB face), `euler_from_matrix_continuous()` (YXZ Euler from a rotation matrix, unwrapped continuous with the previous angle to avoid +/-180 degree flips), `set_angle_from_housing()`/`set_angle_from_cavity()` (align an object's angle3d to a housing's/cavity's world rotation via `model3d.forward_up` face indices), and `reset_angle()` (reset angle3d to identity).

#### rope_pull_handler.py

The peg-board "rope pull" reconciliation solver (`BUNDLE_PLACEMENT.md` sections 6/6b). `resolve_rigid_move` (interactive drag of an anchor/waypoint) and `realize_length_change`/`realize_3d_move` (after a 3D length change) binary-search the largest fraction of a drag's delta every touched chain can accept, cascading through whatever rigid object (Transition, Housing, Splice, WireServiceLoop) each chain's far end is anchored to when its own slack can't absorb the move. A pulled Transition rotates about the arriving branch's own pivot rather than just translating. Waypoint count is held fixed during an in-progress drag (`_commit_points`'s `in_progress` flag) and only reconciled to its real shape once on mouse-up (`finalize_drag`), to avoid DB churn every frame.

⚠ **Bundle/transition-routed wires need protected-point support, added 2026-10-28 (user report + user-specified design).** Confirmed live: once a wire is routed through a bundle (`wire_routing_drag.py`'s cross-view feature, same date), the very next `realize_length_change` call on that wire collapsed its whole peg-board route down to a single untagged point -- `_reconcile_interior`'s reuse-by-position algorithm has no concept of "this point is fixed," only `leading`/`trailing` COUNTS at the two outer ends (built for terminal/cavity stubs, `_stub_boundary`), so a bundle/transition-tagged run sitting in the MIDDLE of the interior got swept up and treated as disposable slack. User's own rule (several corrections, taken together): (1) a point tagged `bundle_id`/`transition_id`/`transition_branch_id`, plus its bracketing guard stub(s), is "etched in stone" to a WIRE's own solve -- never added, removed or repositioned; (2) a bundle's OWN interior waypoints (not its end points, which are anchors and were already safe) ARE removable, but only by the BUNDLE's own solve, which must always run first -- "the wire length is always going to be longer than the bundle length so the bundle is the starting point," no ordering concern between multiple wires sharing one bundle; (3) add/remove for a wire only ever happens in the free run after its exit stub (or before its entry stub) -- there is always such a free run, by construction of the drag-and-drop eligibility rule.
- New `_wire_protected_runs(ptables, wire_db_obj, view)`: every contiguous bundle/transition-tagged run (plus bracketing guards) in a wire's own interior point list for `view`, run against BOTH `'pegboard'` and `'3d'` independently (the two views' own point ids/counts for the SAME logical run can differ) and paired up by ORDER, since both reflect the same view-agnostic entry/hops sequence.
- `_reconcile_interior` now dispatches to new `_reconcile_wire_through_bundle` for a wire with any such run, leaving its OWN single-segment logic (and every bundle/ordinary-wire call) completely untouched -- zero behavior change for the existing cases. The shared reuse/insert/delete core was factored out into `_reconcile_segment` so both paths run the identical, already-proven algorithm.
- `_reconcile_wire_through_bundle` splits the interior into `len(protected_runs) + 1` independent free segments, each solved with its own `solve_chain` call (own anchors -- either the wire's true end or a protected run's own current boundary point, read fresh -- and own required length, computed from the matching stretch of the wire's real 3D path), then splices the untouched protected runs back in between. Terminal/cavity stub exclusion (`_stub_boundary`'s `leading`/`trailing`, same as `_reconcile_interior`) is layered on top and independent of this -- a wire can be attached to a terminal AND routed through a bundle at the same time.
- `_anchors_and_length` also subtracts every protected run's own fixed 3D length (new `_wire_bundle_length3d`) from a wire's available slack budget, exactly like the existing stub-length subtraction, so the OVERALL cascade/feasibility math (pulling a far housing/transition if slack runs out) stays correct -- this one part DOES affect every wire with a bundle/transition run, by design, but only the length NUMBER, not which points are touched.
- New `realize_bundle_and_wires(mainframe, bundle_db_obj, in_progress=False)`: the one new public entry point -- reconciles the bundle first, then every wire in `bundle_db_obj.wires`. `wire_routing_drag.py`'s cross-view routing now calls this (when it touched any bundle) instead of calling `realize_length_change` on just the one wire directly.
- **Closed (mandatory per user, 2026-10-28, same day):** if the bundle's own solve changes its waypoint *count* (slack added/removed a bend), every wire routed through it must reflect that. New `_resync_wire_bundle_span(mainframe, wire_db_obj, bundle_db_obj)` -- finds the wire's own contiguous `bundle_id`-tagged run for that SPECIFIC bundle, checks whether its interior point-id list still matches the bundle's CURRENT `waypoints_pegboard` (no-op, no DB write, if so), and otherwise splices the bundle's current waypoint ids in between the run's own two end points (which never change identity -- anchors) via the same whole-route read/rebuild/`set_route`/reapply-tags dance `wire_routing_handler._commit_route` already uses, never a hand-rolled partial insert/delete. `realize_bundle_and_wires` now calls this for every wire in `bundle_db_obj.wires`, before that wire's own `realize_length_change`, so each wire's own free-segment solve always runs against the bundle's truly current shape.
- **Cavity-seated terminal stubs already worked correctly with no new code needed** (verified, not assumed, 2026-10-28, user asked specifically): `_structural_stub_ids` (confirmed live 2026-10-13, predates this whole feature) already protects both a terminal's own stub point AND, when seated in a cavity, that cavity's own back-of-cavity point, as one combined `leading`/`trailing` exclusion -- `_reconcile_wire_through_bundle` receives and respects that same pair (see its own `leading`/`trailing` parameters), so a wire with both a cavity-seated terminal AND a bundle route together is handled by the existing mechanism, unmodified.
- Not run against the live app's own full matrix yet (multi-transition drag, a wire with a stub AND a bundle together, two bundles on one wire) -- only the single-bundle, no-stub case has been confirmed working end to end.

#### wire_circuit.py

`reconcile_wire_circuit(project, wire)` — works out which circuit a freshly connected wire and the objects on its two ends belong to, reading the ends from `Wire.start_sibling`/`stop_sibling` (so call it only AFTER the ends are attached). No circuit among them and one end is a terminal: `Project.create_circuit()` and the first terminal (start end first) becomes the start terminal. One circuit: the wire and every circuit-less end join it via `PJTCircuit.add_member`. Two or more: a Yes/No dialog, then `Project.merge_circuits` into the start side's circuit; No leaves everything as it was. The decision is made over the WHOLE chain the wire is part of (`PJTCircuitsTable.connected_members`), not only its two ends: a circuit anywhere along it takes part in the Yes/No question, and the chosen circuit then spreads to everything connected (`PJTCircuitsTable.propagate`, via `add_member`). It never removes a member from a circuit, and a wire with only free or layout ends, or only splices, creates nothing. Idempotent. Called from the three add-wire handlers (`add_handlers/editor_3d|editor_pegboard|editor_schematic/wire.py`), `wire_snap.commit_snap` (terminal/splice snap, extension mode and endpoint drags) and `wire_drag_base.merge_wire_into` (every wire-to-wire join). Not covered: deleting a wire, terminal or splice never removes an emptied circuit, and detaching a wire end (`Terminal.detach_wires`) does not touch circuits. `reconcile_wire_circuit` now does two things after a connection: `_reconcile_circuits` (the circuit logic above) and `_replicate_operating_temp`, which spreads the operating temperature across the wire and the objects at its two ends: a part with none takes the value the others hold, and when the parts hold DIFFERENT values a Yes/No dialog asks whether to set them all to the start side's value (No leaves everything, so a harness passing a firewall can keep parts at different temperatures; it asks again on the next connection). The temperature is also carried by `wire_topology.split_wire_at_point` (both halves) and `merge_wires`, the 3D and schematic splice handlers, and the service-loop creation in `objects_3d/wire_service_loop.py`, because those create a part from an existing wire without a connection event.

#### wire_drag_base.py

`WireDragMixin` — the shared segment-local wire drag algorithm (which point(s) a click-drag moves, snap-probe teleport, AWG-mismatch warning-only behavior), used identically by `drag_handlers.editor_3d.wire.Wire` and `drag_handlers.editor_pegboard.wire.Wire`. It deliberately defines **no** `__init__`/`delete`, and only `__call__`, under an explicit-dispatch discipline rather than relying on `super()`/MRO: a mixin combined via plain multiple inheritance has its method resolution decided by base-class order, which was confirmed live (2026-09-13) to silently break — reversing `class Wire(WireDragMixin, DragHandler3D)` to the other order changed which `__init__`/`delete` ran, or dropped the mixin's setup entirely, and `DragHandlerBase.__call__`'s `NotImplementedError` silently won over the mixin's real `__call__` until each concrete `Wire` added its own explicit override. Every concrete `Wire` (`drag_handlers.editor_3d.wire.Wire`/`editor_pegboard.wire.Wire`) calls its real base (`DragHandler3D`/`DragHandlerPegboard`) by name, never `super()` — a confirmed MRO bug, not a style preference.

⚠ **Peg-board waypoints are a completely separate list from 3D waypoints -- adding bends in one view gives you nothing to grab in the other (user report, 2026-10-28).** `WireDragMixin.plan_wire_drag`'s step 2 ("find the segment nearest the click") walks THIS view's own `_waypoints(db_obj)` (`waypoints3d`/`waypoints_pegboard` -- see `objects.mixins.wire.WireTypeMixin`'s own docstring) -- never the other view's. A wire with 2+ interior waypoints in 3D but ZERO in peg-board has, in peg-board, exactly one "segment": the whole body from true start to true stop. Clicking ANYWHERE along that body -- the visual middle included -- always resolves to `bounding = (0, 1)`, i.e. the wire's own two true endpoints, filtered by `is_anchor_point`: one end anchored (a terminal) + one free end means EVERY click anywhere on the body drags that one free end (`snap_point` set, behaves exactly like grabbing "the end") no matter where the user actually clicks; both ends anchored means no drag is possible at all, from any click point. This is correct, documented behavior (see `plan_wire_drag`'s own docstring, point 2: "a plain 2-point wire... a mid-body click moves both ends together" -- only true when BOTH ends are free), not a picking bug -- it just reads as "I can only drag by the ends" when a wire has no peg-board-side bends yet. **The fix is always the same: right-click the wire IN THE VIEW where you need a middle grab-point and "Add Handle" (`WireMenu.on_add_handle` -> `add_handlers.editor_pegboard.wire_layout.create_wire_layout_on_wire_pegboard`, mirrors the 3D `add_handlers.editor_3d.wire_layout` flow) at least twice there, then drag the section BETWEEN those two new waypoints** -- never a section touching the true start/stop (see `wire_routing_drag.py`'s own entry below for why that section specifically is also what `BUNDLE_PLACEMENT.md` section 12's routing feature requires).

#### wire_routing_drag.py

The UI half of the skeleton-first wire routing engine (`BUNDLE_PLACEMENT.md` section 12): drag-start eligibility scan + two-tier highlight (`compute_eligible_targets`/`highlight_eligible`/`set_hover_highlight`), `WireRouteMixin` (mixed into `drag_handlers.editor_3d`/`editor_pegboard.wire_route.WireRoute`, alongside that view's own ordinary `wire.Wire` drag handler), and `RouteSession` (the click-driven continuation through further transitions, installed as a Wire view object's own `_active_handler`). This feature was **dead on arrival from a naming typo** (found and fixed 2026-10-08): `WireRouteMixin._is_routing_eligible` read `self._moving` (a never-written class-level placeholder, copy-paste drift from a different name) instead of `self.moving` (the real attribute `WireDragMixin._arm_drag` in `wire_drag_base.py` actually sets) — since that check started with `if self.end is not None or self._moving is None or ...: return False`, it returned `False` unconditionally, so nothing was ever highlighted and `drop_hit` was never set, for every wire drag in both views. The fix was a simple attribute-name correction (`_moving` -> `moving`, 3 reads), not a logic bug; it is still not verified running end-to-end in the live app.

**Cross-view routing added (user request, 2026-10-28):** dropping a wire onto a bundle/branch in EITHER the 3D or the peg-board view now also routes it through that same bundle in the OTHER view, and reconciles the wire's length (peg-board always conforms to 3D, never the reverse -- same direction BUNDLE_PLACEMENT.md section 6 already uses everywhere else). New `_companion_entry_position`/`_route_companion_view`, called from `begin_route` (immediate-finish case, no hops) and from `RouteSession.__call__`'s `LEFT_UP` branch (multi-transition case -- `RouteSession` now takes and stores the original `entry` and accumulates every `ContinueBranch` hop chosen into `self._hops` as it goes, so the exact same path can be replayed into a second `RouteWalk` for the other view once the first one commits). The companion view's own `grabbed_position` (`RouteWalk` needs one to find which existing section of the wire to splice the detour into, same as a real drag) is the entry's own bundle-end/branch position IN THAT VIEW, found via `Point`'s singleton-by-`db_id` identity (not a guess -- `wire_topology.segment_index` has no proximity threshold, so it always returns a sane nearest-section index regardless of how far that is from the companion view's own route). Ends with `rope_pull_handler.realize_length_change(mainframe, wire_obj.db_obj, in_progress=False)`, the exact same call `drag_handlers/editor_3d/generic.py`'s `_realize_pegboard_length` already uses for every other "a wire's 3D shape changed" case -- induces or removes peg-board slack as needed, refuses (logged, not raised) only if the cascade is genuinely infeasible. `EndAtSplice` hops are explicitly refused (logged) rather than mirrored, since splices aren't wired into this feature at all yet (see this file's own module docstring) and an `EndAtSplice`'s `point_id` is view-specific in a way a `ContinueBranch`'s wrapped row is not. Not run in the live app -- same ceiling as the rest of this module.

**Later that same day:** several real bugs in this cross-view path were found live and fixed (see `MEMORY.md`'s "Cross-view wire routing's real bug"/"two follow-ups" entries, 2026-10-28) -- `_companion_entry` had to be added because reusing the original view's `EnterBundle` verbatim fed the companion `RouteWalk` a point from the wrong view's table (`IndexError` on commit), and `rope_pull_handler._reconcile_wire_through_bundle`/`_resync_wire_bundle_span` had to be built because the plain length-reconciliation call was erasing the very bundle-routed segment this function had just committed. The single-bundle, no-terminal-stub case was confirmed working live after those fixes (the user's own test, 2026-10-28). `_route_companion_view` therefore logs every step at `_logger.error` (not `warning`, which can be config-gated off) deliberately, left in place rather than removed, because this is a genuinely new, narrow, previously-broken-three-times path -- **if a future report says "routes in the view I dragged in but not the other one" again, read the app log for these `wire_routing_drag:` lines FIRST** (they cover: which companion view was picked, the resolved `grabbed_position`, whether `companion_walk` finished, the route it actually committed, and the length-reconciliation step) rather than re-deriving the whole call chain from scratch. Combinations NOT yet exercised live even after those fixes (per that same MEMORY.md entry): a wire with a cavity-seated terminal stub AND a bundle route together, two bundles on one wire, a bare transition pass-through with no bundle, and the multi-transition `RouteSession` path -- a report naming one of these is new territory, not a repeat of an already-fixed bug.

#### wire_routing_handler.py

The skeleton-first wire routing engine itself: `RouteWalk` (resumable, one hop at a time) and `route_wire` (thin wrapper for an already-resolved hop list) walk bundles/transitions, computing guard/housing-breakout-point geometry, and commit to `pjt_wire_paths`. Takes a `view` arg (`'3d'`/`'pegboard'`) throughout — same skeleton topology, each view's own point columns. Every other call site here (`EnterBundle`/`EnterBranch`/`ContinueBranch`/`begin_route`/`is_bundle_end_free`/`wire_fits_bundle`/`wire_fits_branch`/`highlight_branch`/`clear_branch_highlight`/`hit_test_branch_ray`) was cross-checked against `wire_routing_drag.py`'s naming-typo fix above and found clean — all argument counts/order match, no further typos found.

⚠ That cross-check missed a real bug, found + fixed 2026-10-27 on the feature's first live end-to-end run: `RouteWalk.__init__`/`_advance`/`choose` built `self._ordered`/`self._point_tags` from THREE different id sources, two of them wrong. `branch.position3d_id`/`transition.position3d_id`/`pjt_bundle_paths_table.point_ids(...)` (table-column reads) correctly give the raw 16-byte `pjt_points3d`/`pjt_points_pegboard` row id. But `entry.point.db_id` (the `EnterBundle` entry point), `far_point.db_id` (`far_point = bundle.start_position3d`/`stop_position3d`, a `Point`), and `branch_point.db_id` (`branch_point = next_branch.position3d`/`position_pegboard`, also a `Point`) are `geometry.point.Point.db_id` values — per that class's own docstring, the row id with a 2-byte `b'3d'`/`b'2d'` view-tag suffix ALWAYS appended, stripped with `db_id[:-2]` everywhere else in the app that mixes the two (see `add_handlers/editor_3d/bundle.py`, `wire.py`, `splice.py`, `pjt_housing.py`, etc. for the same `[:-2]` pattern). All three sites here were missing that strip, so `commit()`'s `self._points_table[self._ordered[0]]` (and any later lookup using one of these three point ids as a dict/table key) raised `IndexError` on an 18-byte key the table never recognizes. Fixed by slicing `[:-2]` at all three `_add()` call sites.

⚠ Second bug, same first live run, same `_commit_route` (found + fixed 2026-10-27, user report): the two guard `WireLayout`s it creates rendered with the wrong diameter AND color — always `WireLayout.__init__`'s own hardcoded fallback (3.0mm, plain gray plastic), never the actual wire's part. Cause: `_commit_route` built each guard's `objects.wire_layout.WireLayout` view object (which reads `PJTWireLayout.diameter`/the wire's part color ONCE at construction, via `attached_wires` -> `pjt_wire_paths_table.wire_ids_for_point` — see `pjt_wire_layout.py`'s own `diameter` docstring) BEFORE calling `paths_table.set_route(...)` — at that point nothing in `pjt_wire_paths` referenced the new guard point yet, so `attached_wires` found no wire and the view object permanently fell back to the default, with nothing ever re-reading or refreshing it afterward. Fixed by moving `set_route(...)` (and the `detour`/`new_interior_ids` computation it needs) BEFORE the guard-`WireLayout`-creation loop, so the route already exists by the time each guard's view object is constructed and resolves the real wire's diameter/color immediately. No FK/ordering dependency runs the other way (`set_route` only needs the point rows, already inserted earlier, not a `pjt_wire_layouts` row).

#### wire_slack.py

`reconcile` — bows a single waypoint into whichever of a wire's two view-paths (3D or peg-board) is shorter, so both agree on one real `length_mm`. Runs once, right after both real endpoints are known; not a continuous solver (later peg-board dragging stays within that length via the existing per-segment length-budget clamp).

#### wire_slack_handler.py

`WireSlackHandler` — a per-wire safety net, one instance per `objects.wire.Wire` facade, binding callbacks onto every one of that wire's own points (both views) so ANY move of ANY of them re-runs `wire_slack.reconcile`, supplementing (not replacing) the explicit `rope_pull_handler.realize_length_change` call sites that can miss a case (confirmed live: a housing drag didn't enumerate a point that mattered). **`release()` (2026-10-25):** `Wire.delete` calls it, and a released handler unbinds and does nothing. Without it a deleted wire's handler lived on (facade and handler refer to each other, so only the garbage collector frees them) and, bound to the wire's points, reconciled a wire whose row was gone: adding a wire service loop (which deletes the wire it replaces, then moves a point that wire was bound to) raised `KeyError` from `table_data.select`. The `finally` in `_reconcile` also used to skip clearing `_reconciling_any` when `register_pegboard()` raised, which silently turned off every wire's reconcile for the rest of the session; it now clears the flag in an inner `finally`.

#### wire_snap.py

View-agnostic snap helpers used by both the add-session and drag flows — `SnapOverlay` (floating compat-message label), `check_terminal_compat`/`check_splice_compat`/`capacity_warning` (AWG/cross-section compatibility, non-blocking warnings), `resolve_picked`/`get_snap_info` (unwrap a snap-probe hit to the real target), and `snap_point`/`commit_snap` (finalize a connection on release). Deliberately kept separate from `snap_probe_set.py` so code that only needs these helpers never has to import the abstract probe-building machinery. The snap-COMMIT side of a drag's snap (`commit_snap`) is still 3D/2D-only for a snap onto a TERMINAL or SPLICE — `Terminal.add_wire`/`Splice.add_wire` only write `*_position3d`/`*_position2d` columns. The live in-drag teleport-onto-a-probe already works for peg-board (via `SnapProbeSet`'s peg-board subclass); only finalizing that specific case into a real connection on mouse-up does not yet.

#### wire_topology.py

`split_wire_at_point`/`merge_wires` — forking a wire in two around a real junction (splice, wire service loop) and merging two forked wires back into one when the junction is removed. Ordinary bends never go through here (see `wire_layout_handler.py`). Two merges, same result, different method: `merge_wires` makes a NEW wire row spanning `wire_before`'s start to `wire_after`'s stop (new ids; its route rows are re-created by `set_route`, so bundle/transition tags on them are lost) and deletes both originals; `merge_wires_in_place` (2026-10-26, wire service loop delete) keeps `wire_before`'s row and object, repoints its stop ids in every view to `wire_after`'s far end (`obj3d`/`objpegboard`/`objschematic.set_stop_position`), moves `wire_after`'s route rows onto it by changing each row's `wire_id`/`idx` (tags stay), moves its markers and far-end sibling, deletes the emptied `wire_after`, and deletes NO point row (the two points the wires met at just stop being used). **Both take the wires in running order: `wire_before` is the one whose STOP is at the junction, `wire_after` the one whose START is** -- a caller that goes by a junction object's `start_sibling`/`stop_sibling` must not pass them straight through (for a wire service loop `start_sibling` is the wire AFTER it). `Splice.delete` and `WireLayout._reconnect_wires` still call `merge_wires`. `add_handlers.editor_3d.wire_service_loop.restore_wire_from_split` (the cancel-placement path) now calls `merge_wires_in_place(state.wire2, state.wire1)` (`wire2` is the wire BEFORE the loop, `wire1` the one after it) and no longer deletes the two wires again afterwards (it used to pass them to `merge_wires` in the wrong order and then delete both, already deleted by the merge).
